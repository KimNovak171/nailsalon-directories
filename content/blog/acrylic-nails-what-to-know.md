Acrylic nails have been one of the most popular nail enhancement options for decades, and for good reason. They offer length, strength, and versatility that natural nails often cannot match. But they also require a specific commitment of maintenance, cost, and nail care to wear well. Here is a complete guide to what acrylic nails are, how they work, and what to expect.

## What Acrylic Nails Are

Acrylic nails are a nail enhancement created by combining a liquid monomer with a powder polymer to form a malleable substance that is applied over the natural nail or over a nail tip for added length. The mixture hardens through a chemical reaction as it is exposed to air, creating a durable, hard surface that can be shaped, filed, and polished.

The resulting enhancement is significantly harder and more rigid than a natural nail, which makes it resistant to breakage and allows for lengths and shapes that natural nails cannot hold on their own.

## How the Application Process Works

An acrylic appointment at a nail salon typically begins with nail preparation: removing existing polish, lightly buffing the nail surface, and applying a primer to the natural nail to help the acrylic bond. If length is being added, plastic nail tips are glued to the ends of the natural nails before the acrylic is applied.

Your technician then works one nail at a time, dipping a brush into the liquid monomer and the powder polymer to form a small bead of acrylic, which is placed onto the nail and pressed and shaped while it is still malleable. The acrylic hardens within minutes, after which each nail is filed and shaped to the desired length and contour.

A full set of acrylic nails typically takes one to two hours depending on the complexity of the shape and any nail art being added.

## Fill Appointments

One of the most important things to understand about acrylic nails is that they require regular fill appointments every two to three weeks. As your natural nail grows, a gap appears between the base of the acrylic and the cuticle area. A fill appointment addresses this gap by applying fresh acrylic to the new growth area to maintain the seamless look.

Skipping fill appointments allows the gap to widen, which increases the risk of the acrylic lifting and trapping moisture between the enhancement and the natural nail. This moisture environment can encourage bacterial or fungal growth.

## Removal

Acrylic nails should always be removed by a professional or through the proper soak-off method at home. The correct removal process involves soaking the nails in pure acetone until the acrylic softens enough to be gently pushed off the nail without force.

Never attempt to pry, pull, or force acrylic nails off. This tears layers of the natural nail away with the acrylic and can cause significant damage that takes months to grow out.

## Impact on Natural Nails

When applied and removed properly, acrylics do not inherently damage natural nails. However, the combination of buffing the nail surface, repeated acetone removal, and the weight and rigidity of the enhancement over time does affect the natural nail to some degree.

People who wear acrylic nails for extended periods often find that their natural nails are thinner, more brittle, or slower to grow than before they started. Taking breaks from enhancements periodically and focusing on nail health during those breaks helps maintain the condition of the natural nail.

## Cost and Maintenance Budget

A full set of acrylic nails typically costs more than a standard gel manicure, and fill appointments every two to three weeks add up over time. Before committing to acrylics, factor in the ongoing cost of fills plus the occasional full removal and reset when the enhancement needs to be completely redone.

## Who Acrylic Nails Are Best For

Acrylics are an excellent choice for people who want significant length, dramatic shapes like coffin or stiletto that natural nails cannot hold, or extra strength for natural nails that are prone to breaking. They are also a good option for people who bite their nails and want a deterrent and aesthetic improvement while working on the habit.

They require consistent maintenance commitment and a willingness to invest in regular fill appointments to stay looking their best.

## The Bottom Line

Acrylic nails are a long-term commitment that rewards consistent care. Building a relationship with a skilled technician who works with acrylics regularly, following the fill schedule, and maintaining your nails properly between appointments ensures they look their best while protecting your natural nail health throughout.
