Pedicures are one of the most popular and consistently satisfying nail salon services, combining foot care, relaxation, and polish in a single appointment. But the menu of pedicure options at most salons has expanded considerably beyond the simple basic pedicure, and understanding what each type involves helps you choose the right service for your needs and budget.

## Basic Pedicure

A basic pedicure is the standard service that most people think of when they hear the word pedicure. It includes a warm foot soak, nail trimming and shaping, cuticle care, basic callus removal with a pumice stone or foot file, a brief lower leg and foot massage, and nail polish application.

A basic pedicure typically takes 30 to 45 minutes and is the most affordable pedicure option on any salon menu. It covers all the essential maintenance needs for most people and is a perfectly good choice for regular upkeep appointments.

## Gel Pedicure

A gel pedicure is a basic pedicure with gel polish used instead of regular polish for the final color application. The advantage is the same as with a gel manicure — the finish is harder, glossier, and more resistant to chipping than regular polish, making it ideal for people who want their pedicure to look fresh for an extended period.

Gel pedicure polish typically lasts three to four weeks on toenails, which grow and flex less than fingernails. This longer wear time makes gel a popular choice for pedicures even among people who prefer regular polish on their fingernails.

## Spa Pedicure

A spa pedicure builds on the basic pedicure with enhanced treatments and a longer service time. The specific additions vary by salon but typically include a more thorough exfoliation treatment using a sugar or salt scrub, a paraffin wax treatment or a mask applied to the feet and lower legs, an extended massage, and sometimes a hot stone component.

Spa pedicures are focused on relaxation and skin treatment in addition to standard nail care. They typically take 60 to 90 minutes and cost more than a basic pedicure. They are a popular choice for special occasions, self-care days, or as a treat after periods of heavy activity that has been hard on the feet.

## Paraffin Pedicure

A paraffin pedicure incorporates a paraffin wax treatment as its central feature. Paraffin wax is melted to a warm liquid state and the feet are dipped into it or painted with it and then wrapped in plastic bags or warm towels while the wax sets. The heat from the wax opens pores and increases circulation, and the wax itself seals moisture into the skin.

When the wax is peeled away, it takes dead skin cells with it, leaving the feet noticeably softer and smoother. Paraffin pedicures are particularly beneficial for very dry or cracked heels.

## Hot Stone Pedicure

A hot stone pedicure uses smooth, heated stones as a massage tool during the service. The warmth of the stones relaxes muscles and increases circulation in the lower legs and feet, enhancing the massage component of the pedicure significantly.

Hot stone pedicures are deeply relaxing and are a popular upgrade for people who have tired or aching feet and legs, particularly those who stand for long periods in their work or exercise regularly.

## Medical or Medi Pedicure

A medical pedicure, sometimes called a medi pedi, is a therapeutic foot care service that goes beyond cosmetic treatment. It is typically performed by a trained technician with specialized knowledge in foot health and focuses on addressing issues like ingrown nails, calluses, corns, and dry skin more thoroughly than a standard pedicure.

Medical pedicures are often done without polish so the focus remains entirely on foot health rather than aesthetics. They are appropriate for people with specific foot concerns, including those with diabetes, who require more careful and specialized foot care.

## How to Choose

If you want basic maintenance and polish at a reasonable price, a basic pedicure serves the purpose well. If you want a treat and extended relaxation, a spa pedicure with additional skin treatments is worth the upgrade. If foot health is the priority, a medical or therapeutic pedicure is the appropriate choice.

Ask your salon what each pedicure on their menu specifically includes, since the names and contents of pedicure packages vary between salons. Knowing exactly what you are getting ensures the service matches your expectations and needs.

## The Bottom Line

A pedicure matched to your actual needs is far more satisfying than defaulting to the same service every time. Asking your nail technician what they would recommend based on the current condition of your feet is a useful conversation to have at each visit.
